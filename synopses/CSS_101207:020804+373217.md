## Classification

CSS J020804.2+373217 was discovered by the Catalina Real-time Transient Survey (CRTS) as an eruptive cataclysmic variable and is classified as a dwarf nova. The object is associated with the X-ray source 1RXS J020802.6+373236, indicating detectable high-energy emission in addition to its optical variability.

Subsequent spectroscopic follow-up confirmed its cataclysmic-variable nature and established it as a short-period dwarf nova belonging to the population that dominates the region below the cataclysmic-variable period gap.

## Discovery and Outburst History

The CRTS survey identified three recorded outbursts, with brightness varying from approximately magnitude 17.9 in quiescence to 15.5 during outburst.

The object appears in the large Catalina dwarf-nova catalogs and outburst studies used to investigate the statistical properties of eruptive cataclysmic variables. Its repeated eruptions and relatively large outburst amplitude are consistent with a low mass-transfer dwarf nova.

## Orbital Period

One of the most significant results for CSS J020804.2+373217 is the spectroscopic determination of its orbital period. Thorstensen et al. measured:

**Porb = 0.05845 ± 0.00009 days (84.2 ± 0.2 minutes)**.

This places the system well below the 2–3 hour cataclysmic-variable period gap and only slightly above the period minimum. Systems in this regime generally have low mass-transfer rates and faint accretion disks.

## Spectroscopic Properties

Follow-up spectroscopy revealed:

- Strong Balmer emission lines
- Double-peaked line profiles
- Hα equivalent width near 80 Å
- Hα FWHM near 2000 km s⁻¹
- Peak separation around 825 km s⁻¹

The double-peaked line profile indicates an accretion disk viewed at moderate to relatively high inclination. No eclipses have been reported. The spectrum resembles that of a typical short-period dwarf nova observed near quiescence.

## A Peculiar Photometric Signal

An unusual photometric modulation was reported during outburst with a period of approximately 0.0193 days (27.8 minutes). Because the spectroscopic orbital period is firmly established at approximately 84.2 minutes and the optical spectrum is that of a normal hydrogen-rich dwarf nova, the origin of this signal remains uncertain. Follow-up investigators were unable to provide a definitive explanation.

## Evolutionary Context

With an orbital period of only 84 minutes, CSS J020804.2+373217 lies among the shortest-period hydrogen-rich cataclysmic variables known. Gaia-based studies show that systems in this regime occupy the region of the Hertzsprung-Russell diagram closest to the white-dwarf cooling sequence because:

- Donor stars are extremely low mass
- Accretion rates are relatively low
- The white dwarf contributes much of the optical light

Such systems are closely related to SU UMa and WZ Sge-type dwarf novae that dominate the short-period cataclysmic-variable population.

## Scientific Importance

CSS J020804.2+373217 is important because it lies near the cataclysmic-variable period minimum, where evolutionary processes slow and systems accumulate. Studies of such objects help constrain:

- Angular-momentum loss mechanisms
- Evolution toward the period minimum
- Accretion-disk physics in low mass-transfer systems
- The population of highly evolved dwarf novae

The importance of short-period systems similar to CSS J020804.2+373217 has been emphasized in both CRTS population studies and Gaia-era investigations of cataclysmic variables.

## Observing Significance

CSS J020804.2+373217 is an attractive monitoring target because:

- It exhibits recurrent dwarf-nova outbursts
- Future superoutbursts may reveal superhumps and refine system parameters
- The unexplained 27.8-minute photometric signal deserves further study
- Its period places it near an especially informative stage of cataclysmic-variable evolution

Regular monitoring during future outbursts could help determine whether the system belongs to the SU UMa subclass and establish whether superhumps occur.

## Synopsis for Observers

CSS J020804.2+373217 is a short-period dwarf nova with a spectroscopically measured orbital period of 84.2 minutes. Its spectrum exhibits strong double-peaked Balmer emission from an accretion disk, and its location near the cataclysmic-variable period minimum makes it one of the more evolutionarily interesting dwarf novae. The system has experienced multiple recorded outbursts and is associated with a ROSAT X-ray source. Although it appears to be a fairly typical short-period dwarf nova, the unexplained 27.8-minute photometric modulation reported during outburst remains intriguing. Continued monitoring is warranted for both future superoutbursts and long-term investigation of its accretion behavior and evolutionary state.
