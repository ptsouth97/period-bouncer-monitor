## Classification

TCP J00003597+1757408 is a newly discovered cataclysmic variable that underwent its first recorded major outburst in July 2024. Follow-up spectroscopy and time-series photometry obtained immediately after discovery identified the system as a WZ Sagittae-type dwarf nova (UGWZ), a rare subclass of SU UMa-type cataclysmic variables characterized by infrequent, high-amplitude superoutbursts and very low mass-transfer rates.

## Superoutburst Characteristics

The 2024 eruption exhibited:

- Discovery brightness near magnitude 11.9.
- An outburst lasting approximately 21 days.
- Detection of superhumps.
- A post-outburst rebrightening.
- A large amplitude relative to its quiescent brightness.

A possible Stage A superhump period of 0.0587(1) days was reported from coordinated observations involving Brincat, Hambsch, the Vihorlat Observatory team, and Kyoto University observers.

## Rebrightening Behavior

Following the primary decline, the system underwent a rebrightening around 2024 July 29, returning to approximately magnitude 15.1. This behavior further supports its classification as a WZ Sge-type dwarf nova.

## Astrophysical Significance

WZ Sge stars are among the most evolved dwarf novae known and are characterized by:

- Extremely low mass-transfer rates
- Rare superoutbursts separated by years or decades
- Orbital periods near the CV period minimum
- Large amplitude eruptions
- Early and ordinary superhumps
- Rebrightening behavior following outburst

These systems are valuable laboratories for studying late-stage cataclysmic-variable evolution.

## Observing Significance

TCP J00003597+1757408 is an attractive monitoring target because:

- WZ Sge systems often remain dormant for years or decades.
- Future outbursts may be widely separated.
- Early detection can enable Stage A/B/C superhump studies.
- Rebrightening behavior may constrain accretion-disk models.
- Long-term monitoring may reveal orbital modulation or precursor activity.

## Synopsis for Observers

TCP J00003597+1757408 is a recently discovered WZ Sge-type dwarf nova that underwent a major superoutburst in July 2024. The eruption lasted roughly three weeks, exhibited superhumps with a reported Stage A period near 0.0587 days, and was followed by a rebrightening event. Sustained monitoring remains scientifically valuable because future eruptions may be separated by many years.
