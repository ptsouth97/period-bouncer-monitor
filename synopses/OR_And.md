## Introduction

OR And was first reported as a variable by Hoffmeister (1940). Downes (1986) independently identified OR And (KPD 2302+4910) as a likely cataclysmic variable during an ultraviolet-excess survey. Its spectrum exhibited Balmer emission lines together with He II λ4686 emission and a relatively flat Balmer decrement, indicating the presence of a high-excitation accretion-powered emission region typical of cataclysmic variables and inconsistent with a normal Be-star spectrum.

Long-term studies of OR And have shown that the system undergoes extended high and low states characteristic of VY Scl-type cataclysmic variables. Wenzel & Häussler (1990) presented a long-term light curve based on nearly 450 photographic plates and reported alternating bright and low states, including several deep fading episodes and a shorter low state lasting approximately 50 days.

Subsequent spectroscopic observations confirmed the presence of Balmer, He II λ4686, and C III/N III λ4650 emission features and identified OR And as a nova-like cataclysmic variable (Downes et al. 1995).

More recently, ultraviolet studies classified OR And as a nova-like system observed in a high state, where the accretion disc dominates the observed spectrum (Pala et al. 2022).

Barlow et al. (2022) reported a photometric period of 0.125246 d from TESS observations. Subsequently, Bruch (2023) interpreted the same signal as a negative superhump and adopted an orbital period of 0.13569 d. The VSX catalog lists a period of 0.1359 d (3.26 h) for OR And (Watson et al. 2006).

The relationship between these reported periods remains uncertain and motivates further investigation. However, little recent time-series CCD photometry appears to be available. The complex long-term behavior of OR And makes continued photometric and spectroscopic study valuable.
